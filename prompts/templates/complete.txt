3. **Sizing Constraints to Prevent Overfull (CRITICAL):**
   - **Images**: NEVER use `width=0.9\textwidth` or larger. Use these guidelines:
     * Standard images: `width=0.7\textwidth, keepaspectratio`
     * Wide images: `width=0.6\textwidth, keepaspectratio` or smaller

     * Example: `\includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth, keepaspectratio]{{extracted/figure_01.pdf}}`
   - **Tables**: Prevent tables from overflowing:
     * Add `\small` or `\footnotesize` before wide tables
     * For very wide tables, use: `\resizebox{{0.9\\textwidth}}{{!}}{...table content...}`
     * Split tables across multiple slides if too large
   - **Text Content**:
     * Limit to 5-6 bullet points per slide maximum
     * Keep bullet points concise (1-2 lines each)
     * For dense content, use `\small` at the start of the frame
     * Split content-heavy slides into 2-3 slides
   - **Font Size Rules (CRITICAL - READ CAREFULLY):**
     * **NEVER use `\scriptsize` before `\begin{{itemize}}`** - it breaks font hierarchy!
     * Problem: `\scriptsize` before itemize makes second-level items LARGER than first-level items
     * **ONLY use `\small` or `\footnotesize` before itemize** - these maintain proper hierarchy
     * Correct: `\small \begin{{itemize}}` or just `\begin{{itemize}}` (no size modifier)
     * Wrong: `\scriptsize \begin{{itemize}}` ❌
     * If text is too dense, split into multiple slides instead of shrinking fonts
   - **Content Overflow Handling (CRITICAL):**
     * **NEVER force overflowing content into a single slide**
     * If content doesn't fit on one slide, **ALWAYS split into multiple slides**
     * Example: "Results (Part 1 of 2)" and "Results (Part 2 of 2)"
     * Better to have 2-3 clear slides than 1 cramped, overflowing slide
     * Distribution strategies:
       - Group related points together across slides
       - Use logical breaks (e.g., "Qualitative Results" → "Quantitative Results")
       - Split long bullet lists: first 4-5 bullets on slide 1, rest on slide 2
     * Frames with columns: if content overflows, reduce bullets or split columns to separate slides
   - **Frames**: If a frame has too much content:
     * **FIRST: Split into multiple logical frames (preferred solution)**
     * SECOND: Use `\small` (not scriptsize!) to reduce text size slightly
     * THIRD: Remove less important details
     * NEVER: Force everything into one overflowing slide
